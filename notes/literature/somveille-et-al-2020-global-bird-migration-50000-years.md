# Somveille et al. 2020, simulation-based reconstruction of global bird migration

## Citation

Somveille, M., Wikelski, M., Beyer, R.M., Rodrigues, A.S.L., Manica, A., & Jetz, W. (2020). *Simulation-based reconstruction of global bird migration over the past 50,000 years*. Nature Communications, 11, 801.

## Why this paper matters for the project

This paper is highly relevant as broad background because it develops a mechanistic, simulation-based framework for bird migration under changing climate over long timescales. It is not part of the direct methodological lineage of the Skyllas flyway papers, but it matters for how our project may be framed in relation to migration theory, climatic flexibility, and mechanistic modelling.

## Why it belongs in `refs/background/`

This paper is best treated as background rather than core or direct methods because:
- it operates at a global avifauna scale rather than the specific flyway scale of our project
- it is about climate-driven migration system reconstruction over 50,000 years
- it provides conceptual context for flexibility, climatic drivers, and mechanistic migration modelling
- it is not the direct technical template for our H3 edge-cost implementation

## Main relevance from first read

The paper argues that:
- bird migration can be simulated as a mechanistic response to climate and energetic constraints
- migration is shaped by optimisation of energy budgets
- climatic change can reorganize migration systems over time without migration disappearing as a major phenomenon
- migration flexibility is therefore deeply important in the global avifauna

These themes matter for our project because they resonate with our current interest in:
- climate-driven flyway structure
- interannual variability and behavioural flexibility
- mechanistic rather than purely descriptive migration modelling
- broader climatic accessibility beyond one realised route

## Likely value for our sequel

This paper may help us with:
- introduction/discussion framing around climate and migration flexibility
- positioning our project as a mechanistic migration model at a different scale and resolution
- thinking about how much of migration can be explained from climate and energy constraints alone
