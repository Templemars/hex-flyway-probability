# Somveille, Rodrigues and Manica 2018, energy efficiency drives seasonal redistribution

## Citation

Somveille, M., Rodrigues, A.S.L., & Manica, A. (2018). *Energy efficiency drives the global seasonal distribution of birds*. Nature Ecology & Evolution.

## Why this paper matters for the project

This paper is highly relevant background because it appears to articulate a broad global principle that bird seasonal distributions are shaped by energy efficiency. That idea is closely aligned with the conceptual direction of our project, especially where we think about migration as an environmentally constrained redistribution process rather than only as one fixed route.

## Why it belongs in `refs/background/`

This paper is best treated as background because:
- it appears to operate at a broad macroecological / global-distribution scale
- it motivates mechanistic migration thinking through energetic efficiency
- it is conceptually very relevant, but not the direct technical template for our H3 directional edge-cost implementation

## Main relevance from first read

The title and opening indicate a strong claim:
- energy efficiency is a major driver of the global seasonal distribution of birds

That matters directly for our project because our current thinking is also built around trade-offs among:
- movement cost
- climatic conditions
- resource access
- migration structure

## Why this is useful for our sequel

This paper may help us with:
- framing the project around energy-efficient movement under environmental constraints
- linking flyway structure to broader principles of seasonal redistribution
- justifying why a mechanistic movement model should consider gains and costs in common units or comparable cost components

## Relationship to the current project

This paper is not the direct algorithmic ancestor of the flyway model, but it may be part of the broader conceptual lineage behind the migration/energy-efficiency framing that later work builds on.

It may be useful when we discuss:
- why migration should be seen as an optimisation problem
- how route structure relates to seasonal redistribution
- how broad energetic logic connects to the more specific wind-food-distance edge-cost model
