# Somveille et al. 2021, a general theory of avian migratory connectivity

## Citation

Somveille, M., Bay, R.A., Smith, T.B., Marra, P.P., & Ruegg, K.C. (2021). *A general theory of avian migratory connectivity*. Ecology Letters, 24, 1848–1858.

## Why this paper matters for the project

This paper looks highly relevant because it develops an explicit theoretical framework for avian migratory connectivity based on optimisation of energy expenditure and energy acquisition under intraspecific competition. That is conceptually very close to our broader interest in directional movement, climatic constraints, and mechanistic migration modelling.

## Why it belongs in `refs/methods/`

This paper is more than broad background because it provides:
- a formal modelling framework
- an optimisation-based view of migratory connectivity
- explicit links between energy efficiency, competition, and movement structure

At the same time, it is not part of the direct technical lineage of the Skyllas flyway papers. So `methods` is the right category.

## Main relevance from first read

The core idea is that migratory connectivity can be understood through an **optimal redistribution** of individuals between seasonal grounds, driven by:
- minimisation of energetic migration costs
- optimisation of energy acquisition
- intraspecific competition for resources

This is important for us because it suggests a stronger theoretical framing for our work:
- we are not only fitting flyways
- we may be building a mechanistic movement model embedded in resource and movement trade-offs

## What seems most relevant for our sequel

This paper may be especially useful for:
- framing the migration model mechanistically
- thinking carefully about optimisation language
- discussing how movement costs and resource constraints jointly shape migratory structure
- strengthening the conceptual connection between local movement rules and larger-scale migratory connectivity

## Potential relation to our project

There are clear conceptual links to our current direction:
- our H3 graph is directional and cost-based
- we are combining environmental drivers and movement costs explicitly
- we are interested in how migration structure emerges under climatic constraints

The difference is that our project is currently more route / graph / flyway focused and environmentally explicit, whereas this paper appears to focus more on a general optimisation theory of seasonal redistribution.
