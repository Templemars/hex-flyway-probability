# Skyllas et al. 2023, Arctic tern flyways and changing Atlantic winds

## Citation

Skyllas, N., Loonen, M.J.J.E., & Bintanja, R. (2023). *Arctic tern flyways and the changing Atlantic Ocean wind patterns*. Climate Change Ecology, 6, 100076.

## Why this paper matters for the new project

This paper is highly relevant background for the new hex-grid flyway project because it focuses directly on arctic tern flyways, wind support, crosswind responses, population differences, and climate-related variation in the Atlantic Ocean. It is especially relevant for the parts of the new project dealing with interannual variability, wind-driven flyway structure, and behavioural flexibility.

## Main relevance

The abstract suggests that:
- wind is a major flyway-shaping factor
- different tern populations respond differently to wind and crosswind
- Svalbard and Dutch populations may differ in their adjustment and compensation strategies
- climate change and changing Atlantic wind patterns matter for flyway structure

These points connect strongly to the new project's themes:
- flyway stability under changing climate
- behavioural flexibility or changing movement priorities
- population-specific responses
- climate as a boundary-setting force on migration space

## Likely role in the project

This paper should probably be treated as a high-priority background or near-core reference because it links:
- tern tracking data
- wind structure
- climatic change
- behavioural response

It may help justify:
- focusing first on one population
- keeping wind central in the first model hierarchy
- discussing behavioural flexibility carefully rather than overclaiming direct behavioural inference from model weights
