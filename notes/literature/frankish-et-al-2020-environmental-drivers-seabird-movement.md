# Frankish et al. 2020, environmental drivers of movement in a threatened seabird

## Citation

Frankish, C.K., Phillips, R.A., Clay, T.A., Somveille, M., & Manica, A. (2020). *Environmental drivers of movement in a threatened seabird: insights from a mechanistic model and implications for conservation*. Diversity and Distributions, 26, 1315–1329.

## Why this paper matters for the project

This paper looks highly relevant methodologically because it applies a mechanistic movement model to seabird tracks and explicitly examines the relative roles of:
- wind
- chlorophyll-a (food proxy)
- movement trajectories
- life-history differences

That is strikingly close to the ingredients and questions of our current project.

## Why it belongs in `refs/methods/`

This paper belongs in methods because:
- it deals directly with mechanistic movement modelling
- it uses wind and chlorophyll as environmental drivers
- it tests environmental explanations against observed movement patterns
- it is much closer to our implementation logic than broad macroecological background papers

## Main relevance from first read

From the abstract and introduction, the key ideas are:
- juvenile and adult seabirds can be driven by different environmental mechanisms
- juvenile trajectories may be predicted more strongly by prevailing winds
- adults may be more attracted to productive food-rich areas
- a mechanistic movement model can be used to test competing environmental drivers of movement patterns

## Why this is useful for our sequel

This paper may be especially important for:
- thinking about how to compare alternative movement drivers explicitly
- understanding how wind and food may contribute differently to route structure
- framing mechanistic model evaluation against tracking data
- thinking about behavioural flexibility or age/stage differences in movement strategy

## Strong connection to our project

This is one of the closest papers so far to our current modelling problem because our H3 sequel also aims to build movement from:
- wind structure
- food proxy
- directional local transitions
- explicit algorithmic rules

Even though the exact model structure may differ, this paper looks highly relevant for how we compare environmental mechanisms and interpret model outputs.
